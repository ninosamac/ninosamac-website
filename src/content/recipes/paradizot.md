description: >-
  Dalmatian floating islands: clouds of poached meringue on chilled vanilla
  custard, known as šnenokle inland.
hero: ./paradizot.webp
heroCredit: "Photo: Juhan Harm, CC BY 2.0, via Wikimedia Commons"
servings: 6
prepMinutes: 25
cookMinutes: 20
ingredients:
  - 4 eggs, separated
  - 500 ml whole milk
  - 1 vanilla pod, or 2 tsp vanilla extract
  - 100 g sugar, divided
  - 1 tbsp cornstarch (optional, for a firmer custard)
  - A pinch of salt
  - Grated dark chocolate or caramel, to serve (optional)
steps:
  - Heat the milk with the vanilla (split pod, seeds scraped in) in a wide shallow pan until steaming but not boiling.
  - Whisk the egg whites with the salt to soft peaks. Add 50 g of the sugar a spoonful at a time and whisk to stiff, glossy peaks.
  - With two wet spoons, shape egg-sized ovals of meringue and lower them into the hot milk, a few at a time. Poach for 1–2 minutes per side, turning gently, until just set. Lift out with a slotted spoon onto a rack or clean towel.
  - Strain the milk and discard the vanilla pod. Whisk the yolks with the remaining 50 g sugar (and cornstarch, if using) until pale, then slowly whisk in the warm milk.
  - Return the custard to the pan over low heat and stir constantly for 5–8 minutes until it thickens enough to coat the back of a spoon. Do not let it boil or it will scramble.
  - Strain into a bowl, cool, then chill for at least 2 hours. Pour into shallow bowls, float the meringues on top and finish with chocolate or caramel.

Keep the milk below a simmer, or the meringues puff up and then collapse. The
custard thickens a little more as it chills, so pull it off the heat while it is
still pourable. Dalmatians call it paradižot (or paradižet); in the continental
part of Croatia it is šnenokle. Best the same day — the meringues weep if kept
overnight.
