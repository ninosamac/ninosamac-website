  Clear beef soup built on bone-in shank and root vegetables, simmered low and
  skimmed patiently, finished with thin noodles.

servings: 6
prepMinutes: 20
cookMinutes: 180
ingredients:
  - 1 kg beef shank or short ribs, bone-in
  - 1 marrow bone (optional, for extra richness)
  - 3 liters cold water
  - 2 carrots, peeled
  - 1 parsley root, peeled
  - 1/4 small celeriac, peeled
  - 1 onion, unpeeled, halved
  - 1 leek, white part only
  - 1 bay leaf
  - 6 black peppercorns
  - 2 tsp salt, or to taste
  - 150 g thin soup noodles (fidelini) or vermicelli
  - chopped parsley, for serving
steps:
  - Put the beef and bone in a large pot with the cold water. Bring to a boil over medium heat, uncovered.
  - As it comes to a boil, a grey foam will rise. Skim it off patiently over 10–15 minutes until the liquid runs clear — this is what keeps the broth from turning cloudy.
  - Char the cut side of the onion halves directly on a dry pan or under the broiler until dark brown in spots. This deepens the broth's color without any roux.
  - Add the onion, carrots, parsley root, celeriac, leek, bay leaf and peppercorns to the pot. Lower the heat so the surface barely trembles.
  - Simmer uncovered, skimming occasionally, for 2½–3 hours, until the meat is fork-tender and falls easily from the bone.
  - Strain the broth through a fine sieve into a clean pot. Discard the bone and onion; slice the carrots for serving if you like, and set the meat aside for a second course.
  - Season the broth with salt to taste. Bring back to a gentle boil and cook the noodles directly in it until just tender.
  - Ladle into bowls and scatter with chopped parsley.

This is the broth that opens a Sunday lunch, usually followed by the boiled
beef itself with horseradish or a spoonful of mustard. The whole dish depends
on patience at two points: skimming the foam at the very start, and keeping
the simmer so gentle that the surface barely moves — a hard boil clouds the
broth and toughens the meat. Charring the onion is an old trick for color and
a faint sweetness, without resorting to a roux or bouillon cube. Leftover
broth freezes well and makes a fine base for risotto or a quick rice soup.
