title: Sos bolognese
date: 2026-10-11
description: >-
  Slow-cooked ragù alla bolognese with a soffritto base, a splash of milk and
  wine, and a lazy two-hour simmer.
hero: ./sos-bolognese.webp
heroCredit: "Photo: Alex Sirac, CC BY 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons"
servings: 6
prepMinutes: 25
cookMinutes: 150
ingredients:
  - 2 tbsp olive oil
  - 50 g pancetta, finely diced (optional)
  - 1 large onion, finely chopped
  - 2 carrots, finely chopped
  - 2 celery stalks, finely chopped
  - 3 cloves garlic, minced
  - 500 g minced beef (or half beef, half pork)
  - 150 ml dry red or white wine
  - 2 tbsp tomato paste
  - 400 g canned crushed tomatoes
  - 250 ml beef stock or water
  - 100 ml whole milk
  - 1 bay leaf
  - Salt and black pepper
  - 500 g fresh egg tagliatelle, to serve
  - Freshly grated Parmesan, to serve
steps:
  - Heat the oil in a wide heavy pot over medium heat. Fry the pancetta until the fat renders, then add the onion, carrot and celery with a pinch of salt. Cook 10–12 minutes until soft and sweet, not browned. Add the garlic for the last minute.
  - Raise the heat to medium-high, add the mince and break it up. Let it brown properly, 8–10 minutes, stirring only now and then, until the liquid has cooked off and it starts to stick.
  - Pour in the wine and cook until almost fully evaporated. Stir in the tomato paste and cook for 2 minutes.
  - Add the crushed tomatoes, stock and bay leaf. Bring to a bare simmer, partly cover, and cook on the lowest heat for 1.5–2 hours, stirring occasionally and adding a splash of water if it gets dry.
  - Stir in the milk and simmer uncovered for another 15–20 minutes until the sauce is thick and glossy. Remove the bay leaf and adjust the salt and pepper.
  - Cook the pasta until just shy of al dente. Drain, reserving a cup of the water, and toss it in the pot with the sauce and a splash of pasta water for a minute. Serve with Parmesan.

Always tagliatelle, never spaghetti — the wide, rough ribbons hold the sauce,
which just slides off thin strands. Pappardelle works too. Cook it low and slow;
the sauce should barely blip. It freezes well and is better the next day.
