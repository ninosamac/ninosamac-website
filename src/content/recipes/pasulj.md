description: >-
  Serbian pinto bean stew in a thick, paprika-red broth, cooked low and slow
  with smoked ribs and dried peppers.
hero: ./pasulj.webp
heroCredit: "Photo: Petar Milošević, CC BY-SA 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons"
servings: 6
prepMinutes: 20
cookMinutes: 120
ingredients:
  - 500 g dried pinto beans (šareni pasulj) or white beans, soaked overnight
  - 500 g smoked pork ribs or smoked bacon, in chunks
  - 3 tbsp oil or lard
  - 2 large onions, finely chopped
  - 4 cloves garlic, minced
  - 1 carrot, diced
  - 1 tbsp sweet paprika
  - 1 tsp hot paprika (optional)
  - 2 bay leaves
  - 2 dried red peppers (optional)
  - 1.5 litres water
  - 1 tsp salt, plus more to taste
  - Black pepper
  - Chopped parsley and raw red onion, to serve
steps:
  - Drain the soaked beans and put them in a pot with the water. Bring to the boil, simmer for 10 minutes, then drain and discard the water. This makes them easier to digest. Return the beans to the pot with fresh hot water.
  - Add the smoked ribs, bay leaves and the carrot. Bring to a simmer and cook gently for 1 hour, with the lid slightly ajar, skimming now and then.
  - Heat the oil in a pan and cook the onions for 12–15 minutes until deep golden. Add the garlic for one minute, take the pan off the heat and stir in both paprikas. Scrape it all into the beans.
  - Add the dried peppers, if using, and cook for another 30–45 minutes until the beans are creamy and tender and the broth is thick. Mash a ladleful of beans against the side of the pot to thicken the broth, and add hot water if it gets too dry.
  - Season with salt and pepper only now, then rest for 15 minutes.
  - Serve hot with parsley, raw onion, bread and pickled peppers.

Pasulj is the Serbian counterpart of grah, with the same beans and the same
smoked ribs, but more paprika and a thicker broth. Pinto beans give a creamier
result, but any large white bean works. Salt only at the end, since salt makes
the skins tough. Serve with raw onion and rakija for the full effect.
