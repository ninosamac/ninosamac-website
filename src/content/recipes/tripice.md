  Croatian tripe stew — beef tripe simmered slowly with onion, paprika, potatoes
  and a little tomato until soft and rich.

servings: 6
prepMinutes: 30
cookMinutes: 180
ingredients:
  - 1 kg cleaned beef tripe (already blanched, if you can buy it)
  - 3 tbsp oil or lard
  - 3 onions, finely chopped
  - 4 cloves garlic, minced
  - 2 carrots, diced
  - 1 tbsp sweet paprika
  - 1 tsp hot paprika (optional)
  - 2 tbsp tomato paste
  - 2 bay leaves
  - 1 tsp dried marjoram or a few sprigs of fresh thyme
  - 800 ml beef stock or water
  - 3 medium potatoes, peeled and cut into large chunks
  - Salt and black pepper
  - Chopped parsley, to finish
  - Crusty bread, to serve
steps:
  - Rinse the tripe well and put it in a pot of cold water with a little salt. Bring to the boil, simmer for 10 minutes, drain and rinse again. This removes the smell.
  - Cut the tripe into strips about 1 cm wide and 5 cm long.
  - Heat the oil in a heavy pot and cook the onions over medium heat for 12–15 minutes until soft and golden. Add the garlic and carrots for 2 minutes.
  - Take the pot off the heat, stir in the paprika and tomato paste, then return to the heat and add the tripe, bay leaves and marjoram. Stir for 2 minutes so everything is coated.
  - Pour in the stock, bring to a simmer, cover and cook on low heat for 2 hours, stirring now and then, until the tripe is nearly tender.
  - Add the potatoes and a little more water if the pot looks dry. Cook for another 30–40 minutes until the potatoes are soft and the tripe is tender with a thick, glossy sauce.
  - Season with salt and pepper, scatter with parsley, and serve hot with bread.

Tripice is slow, cheap, cold-weather food, and it takes patience: tripe needs
hours before it turns tender, and the stew is better the next day. Buy tripe
that is already cleaned and blanched, and boil it once more at home. Take the
pot off the heat when you stir in the paprika so it does not burn and turn
bitter.
