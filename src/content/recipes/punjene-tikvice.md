---
title: Punjene tikvice
date: 2026-11-09
description: >-
  Zucchini stuffed with minced meat and rice, baked in a sweet tomato sauce —
  the summer cousin of punjene paprike.
hero: ./punjene-tikvice.webp
heroCredit: "Photo: Schnobby, CC BY-SA 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons"
servings: 6
prepMinutes: 40
cookMinutes: 60
ingredients:
  - 6 medium zucchini (about 1.2 kg), or round zucchini
  - 500 g minced meat (half beef, half pork)
  - 1 large onion, finely chopped
  - 3 cloves garlic, minced
  - 80 g long-grain rice, rinsed
  - 1 egg
  - 2 tbsp finely chopped parsley
  - 1 tsp sweet paprika
  - 2 tbsp neutral oil
  - 500 ml passata or crushed tomatoes
  - 200 ml water or light stock
  - 1 bay leaf
  - 1 tsp sugar
  - Salt and black pepper
steps:
  - Cut the zucchini in half lengthways (or cut the tops off round ones) and scoop out the flesh with a spoon, leaving a shell about 1 cm thick. Chop the scooped flesh finely and squeeze out the water.
  - Soften the onion in the oil over medium heat for 8–10 minutes, adding the chopped zucchini flesh for the last 3 minutes. Stir in the garlic and paprika for 30 seconds, then cool slightly.
  - Mix with the minced meat, rice, egg, parsley, 1½ tsp salt and plenty of pepper. Do not overwork the filling.
  - Salt the zucchini shells lightly inside. Fill them generously, with a small mound on top, and pack them snugly in a baking dish.
  - Mix the passata, water, bay leaf, sugar and a pinch of salt and pour it around, not over, the zucchini. It should come about a third of the way up the sides.
  - Cover with foil and bake at 180 °C for 35 minutes. Uncover and bake for another 20–25 minutes until the zucchini are soft and the filling is cooked through, basting with the sauce once or twice.
  - Rest for 10 minutes. Adjust the salt and sugar in the sauce and serve with mashed potatoes or bread.

Zucchini give off a lot of water, so squeezing the scooped flesh and salting the
shells keeps the sauce from turning thin. The rice only half-cooks in the
filling and finishes in the sauce, so use a little less than for peppers. Small,
firm zucchini are best, because large ones turn watery and bitter. Better the
next day.
